# Pressure Injury Prevention SOP

## 1. Purpose

This synthetic demonstration protocol supports AG-13 testing for pressure-injury prevention workflows. It is not approved for live patient-care use.

## 2. Risk Assessment

Patients should be assessed for pressure-injury risk according to the hospital's approved assessment process.

Risk assessment should consider factors such as:

- reduced mobility
- prolonged bed rest
- impaired sensation
- poor nutritional status
- moisture exposure
- existing skin damage
- use of medical devices

Patients identified as being at increased risk should have preventive measures documented and communicated to the care team.

## 3. Skin Inspection

Skin should be inspected regularly according to hospital policy.

Pay particular attention to:

- bony prominences
- areas exposed to pressure
- skin under or around medical devices
- areas affected by moisture
- existing redness or skin breakdown

Any abnormal skin finding should be documented and escalated according to hospital procedure.

## 4. Repositioning

Patients who are unable to reposition themselves should receive appropriate repositioning assistance according to the approved care plan.

Staff should:

1. Follow the patient's authorised repositioning plan.
2. Avoid unnecessary friction and shear.
3. Use appropriate assistance or equipment where required.
4. Document repositioning according to hospital policy.
5. Escalate concerns when repositioning cannot be performed safely.

AG-13 must not independently create a patient-specific repositioning schedule.

## 5. Support Surfaces

Patients at increased risk may require approved pressure-relieving equipment or support surfaces according to hospital policy.

Examples may include:

- pressure-relieving mattresses
- approved cushions
- positioning aids

Selection of support equipment should follow the authorised clinical or nursing process.

## 6. Moisture and Skin Care

Keep the patient's skin clean and dry according to the approved nursing care plan.

Where moisture exposure is present:

- provide appropriate skin care
- reduce prolonged exposure to moisture
- use approved protective products where required
- document significant skin changes

## 7. Nutrition and Hydration

Nutrition and hydration concerns that may increase pressure-injury risk should be identified and escalated through the authorised clinical pathway.

AG-13 must not prescribe nutritional treatment.

## 8. Medical Device Pressure

Medical devices should be checked for pressure on the skin.

Staff should:

- inspect skin around and beneath devices where appropriate
- ensure devices are positioned securely
- avoid unnecessary pressure
- escalate skin damage or device-related concerns

## 9. Documentation

Document relevant pressure-injury prevention activities according to hospital policy.

Documentation may include:

- risk-assessment findings
- skin observations
- preventive measures
- repositioning
- equipment used
- escalation
- changes in skin condition

## 10. Escalation

Escalate according to hospital policy when:

- new skin breakdown is identified
- existing skin damage worsens
- preventive measures cannot be implemented
- medical devices are causing pressure damage
- the patient's risk status changes significantly
