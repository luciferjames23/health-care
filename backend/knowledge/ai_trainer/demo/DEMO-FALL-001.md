# Patient Fall Prevention SOP

## 1. Fall Risk Assessment

Assess each patient's risk of falling using the hospital-approved assessment process. Review relevant history, mobility, balance, cognition, current condition, and other factors specified by local policy. Reassess when the patient's condition or care setting changes. Communicate identified risks to the care team and document the assessment and agreed precautions.

## 2. Environmental Safety

Check the patient's surroundings for fall hazards. Keep the floor dry and the walking route clear, provide adequate lighting, and make sure needed personal items and the call device are within reach. Check that the bed is positioned safely and that mobility aids are available and in usable condition. Report and address hazards according to hospital procedure.

## 3. Patient Assistance

Explain the assistance available and encourage the patient to request help before moving when assistance is indicated. Assist with transfers and walking according to the patient's assessed needs and hospital policy. Use prescribed or approved mobility aids and involve trained staff when required. Keep the call device accessible and communicate changes in assistance needs to the care team.

## 4. Post-Fall Response

After a patient fall, stay with the patient and summon appropriate clinical assistance. Assess the patient's condition within staff training and follow the hospital's post-fall assessment and escalation procedure. Do not move the patient when injury is suspected unless there is an immediate safety need or the approved procedure directs otherwise. Notify the responsible clinician and follow local policy for observation, communication, and further care.

## 5. Documentation

Document the circumstances and time of the fall, relevant observations and assessment findings, people notified, actions taken, and the patient's response according to hospital policy. Complete the required incident report and communicate follow-up needs to the care team. Record any revised fall-risk assessment and prevention plan in the authorised clinical record.
